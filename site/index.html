EXPERIMENT OUTCOME · OCTOBER 21, 2026

Failed: sound quality versus DSP cost

The sample-based design needed additional FM/VA layers and auxiliary oscillators to supply body, harmonics and movement. Their processing cost accumulates even with a shared filter and envelope. The current 64 sounds remain unapproved by listening, while the stress-load estimate exceeds the real-time budget. This is the failure of this implementation and its intended workload, not a claim that all sample-based synthesis is infeasible.

Time budget: Synthetic 8-melody-voice / 6-drum / 4-FX load, Analog127 and maximum available source gains gives a conditional mean of 3.356–5.114 ms per block against a 2.902 ms deadline: 116–176% of the time budget, not CPU utilization. All 256 full-source profile/phase/register conditions cross the deadline under the primary model assumptions.

Added-source cost: Auxiliary-on/off controls for 16 profiles add 0.177–0.646 ms conditionally, with 5.4–26.9% more host CPU0 synthesis time. This isolates the auxiliary-source increment, not the complete cost of every layer used to supplement PCM. The PCM-free new 32 profiles still predict 3.702–4.996 ms; removing samples alone is not a demonstrated solution.

Evidence: 1,152 host executions: 1,008 candidate runs plus 144 bracketing original runs, calibrated to 11 historical MC-1 captures containing 90,112 measured blocks. Render parity, sanitizers and model regression checks passed, but those checks do not establish approved sound quality or real-time feasibility.

Limits: These are historical-device-calibrated host estimates, not current-firmware measurements on FM-1. No current-device underrun test or safe-polyphony limit was established. Ordinary factory playback and lower-load configurations were not characterized by this stress experiment.

SOUND PACK

Small engine. Many roles.

Each profile combines its sources under a shared pitch and envelope. IDs 08–11 use 1.2-second piano recordings without a default saw body. ID 25 is Strings, using a licensed SoundFont sample: raise ATTACK for a slower onset.

IDs 32–43 are 80S SYNTH, 44–53 are 90S SYNTH, and 54–63 are 00S SYNTH. The new sounds run parallel FM + VA, FM + VA + FM2, or VA + FM + VA2, with independent auxiliary phase and one shared filter and amplitude envelope. These are not three full engines or clones of seven-saw, hard-sync, or PWM synths.

These are design goals, not claims of realistic instrument reproduction or approved sound quality. The files below are host renders from the shared C core. Hardware testing and listening approval are still pending.
